CS5478 Project Proposal

Vision-Guided, Contact-Rich Connector Insertion with Impedance Control

Motivation

Plugging connectors into ports is still one of the least automated steps in electronics assembly.

Demand for servers and data centres is rising, and automating it would cut lead time and raise throughput.

Problem

Using a robot arm and an overhead camera, identify, grasp and insert two different connectors into their matching ports on a board.

Each connector fits only its own port, with just 1-3 mm of clearance.

Why it's hard

Perception and grasp errors (a few mm) can exceed the clearance (1-3 mm)

Stiff position control: lands on the rim and jams
Impedance control + spiral search: finds the port and inserts
Our approach
PerceiveColour + depth segmentation, classical pose estimationOverhead RGB-D camera finds the board and both parts, and identifies which goes where
→
GraspAntipodal grasp from the top-face outlineGrip across opposite sides; turn lying pegs upright in the hand
→
MoveDamped least-squares IK, quintic trajectoriesInverse kinematics and smooth trajectories to above the port
→
InsertCartesian impedance control, force-triggered spiral searchImpedance control: stiff downward, soft sideways. Spiral search with a wrist force sensor if it misses
Relevant literature

Chhatpar & Branicky, 2001Search Strategies for Peg-in-Hole Assemblies with Position Uncertainty→ our force-triggered spiral search
Nguyen, 1988Constructing Force-Closure Grasps→ our antipodal grasp planner

Task and evaluation
Prototype scene

Two parts, one randomly placed board

  • Round peg and square peg
  • Success: both seated, ≤ 60 s each, ≤ 30 N
  • Clearance 1–3 mm, pose error up to 5 mm
  • Port friction 0.3 and 0.8, camera noise on/off
  • Many seeded trials per condition
  • Metrics: success rate, contact force, cycle time, perception error, which stage failed
